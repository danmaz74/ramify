# ramify.ts

<p className="hero-tagline">Multi-file hierarchical modules for TypeScript</p>

TypeScript is modular at exactly one granularity: the file. Above it, every
exported symbol is importable from every file in the codebase, and the
architecture of an application - its domains, capabilities, boundaries - has
no checked existence.

**ramify.ts is a toolkit to define, enforce, and visualize multi-file modules, and
to help agents and humans adhere to its rules.**

It gives a group of files what a single file
already has, a boundary and an interface, and arranges these modules in a
hierarchy, so that work, reasoning, and agent context can split cleanly along the
boundaries at different abstraction levels.

[**Why modularity**](/modularity) makes the full case.

## The ramify modularity model

The modularity model is based on very few simple but powerful rules.

Every cross-module import is closed by default. A module shares a symbol
through exactly two channels - to its parent, or to its own subtree - and
long reach only exists as a chain of such one-hop decisions. That is the
whole model: this nine-module app's entire access policy is the seven dots
in its diagram.

The diagram is live: click a traced symbol to follow its chain.
[**The core model**](/model) builds this picture up from scratch, one step
at a time.

There are two common needs which require more than this: **testing** and
**browser safety**. ramify.ts solves both using
[**tags and availability rules**](/tags).
