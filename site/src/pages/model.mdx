# The core model

The ramify core model is a small set of rules for managing a hierarchical,
multi-file module system in TypeScript, as explained in
[why modularity](/modularity). The concepts are formally defined in the
[glossary](/glossary).

This page explains the model in its simplest form through examples. The more
complex use cases require tags, which are explained in
[their own page](/tags).

## Decomposing a simple example app

The example app has two different domains: `invoicing` and `shipping`. They
share some concepts, but otherwise they're mostly independent and deserve
their own modules. One shared concept is that of Money, and that's why we
created a `globalLibrary` module which contains a `moneyUtils` module, with a
`computeTotal` function which we want to make globally available.

Each domain then decomposes internally. `invoicing` keeps its own shared
vocabulary in an `invoicingLibrary` module, whose `InvoiceModel` is used by
both `invoiceComputation` and `invoicePDF`. `shipping` delegates its hardest
problem to a `routingOptimization` sub-module, which implements
`optimizeRoute` against the `ShipmentPlan` type that `shipping` itself
defines. That gives us nine modules and four symbols, and the diagram below
shows every decision that makes their sharing legal. (Symbols are
self-explanatory, with `PascalCase` for types and `camelCase` for values.)

### How to read it

Every box is a [**module**](/glossary#ramify-module): a directory declared as
a module root. Boxes nest, so modules form a **tree** - a module's parent is
the innermost module containing it - and every source file
[**belongs**](/glossary#files-belonging-to-a-module) to exactly one module.
Inside a module, ramify changes nothing: files export and import exactly as
TypeScript always did. When a file exports a symbol, the module it belongs to
[**owns**](/glossary#module-owned-symbol) that symbol - the first thing each
box lists.

Crossing a module boundary is where the model begins, and its whole content
is one rule: a file may import a symbol exactly when the symbol is
[**available**](/glossary#module-available-symbol) in the file's module. A
module's own symbols are always available in it; everything else is **closed
by default**, and becomes available only if some module
[**exposes**](/glossary#module-exposed-symbol) it. Exposure comes in exactly
two forms - a module may expose any symbol available in it **to its parent**
(available one level up) or **to its descendants** (available in its whole
subtree). Those are the only decisions the model knows. Each one is drawn as
a **filled dot**, and there are only seven dots in this picture for nine
modules: those seven decisions are the entire access policy of the
application, and complexity is measured in decisions, not boxes. What a
symbol's travels earn it shows up in the second thing each box lists - what
is **exposed to it**, by a direct child (named `from`) or by a proper
ancestor (named `granted by`) - so a symbol's reach can be scanned box by
box.

Notice what a decision _cannot_ say. It never names a sibling, never the
root, never "everyone": a module only addresses its parent or its own
subtree, about symbols it already has. Every decision is **local**, and long
reach exists only as a **chain** of one-hop decisions, each made by a module
the symbol had reached.

The diagram is live. **Click any traced symbol**, in the legend or on a row
that lists it, to isolate its chain: everything else dims, the symbol's flows
move the way its exposures travel, and its arrivals blink in every box it
reached. Click the background to clear the selection. The figure also pans
and zooms: drag to move around, scroll over it to zoom about the pointer, and
the buttons in the top-right corner step the zoom or restore the whole
picture.

### One decision, three reaches

Here is what became of our decisions. Three leaf modules make the identical
decision - expose one symbol to their parent - and end up with three
different reaches. `computeTotal` is passed up again and turned downward at
`app`, so every module may import it. `InvoiceModel` is turned downward one
level lower, at `invoicing`, so its two consumers share it and nothing
outside that subtree can. `optimizeRoute` is composed by `shipping` and
exposed no further, so it goes one hop and stops. None of the three owners
decided that; the modules above them did. Exposing a symbol to the parent
cedes its onward routing.

A fourth symbol goes the other way. `shipping` owns `ShipmentPlan` and
exposes it to its descendants: `routingOptimization` consumes a
`ShipmentPlan` and exposes `optimizeRoute` back up, so one edge carries a
type flowing down and a function flowing up - each its own decision.

A "global library" and a "domain library" are the same mechanism at different
altitudes. `app` exposes `computeTotal` to its descendants and `invoicing`
exposes `InvoiceModel` to its descendants; the difference between
application-wide and domain-wide reach is which module made the identical
decision, and nothing is ever declared global. The root is an ordinary module
whose subtree happens to be everything - it owns no code at all here, and
still carries the application's vocabulary.

There is no sibling channel. `shipping` may not import `InvoiceModel`: a
grant never leaves the granter's subtree, and `invoicing` exposed the symbol
to no parent, so nothing outside its subtree ever sees it. The mirror case is
the same rule read the other way - `invoicing` may not import `optimizeRoute`
either, because `shipping` exposed it nowhere. Select either symbol and the
absence is the picture: the flow stops, and no arrival ever blinks in the
sibling's branch. Sharing between siblings is never the exposer's decision;
the route runs through the parent, and the parent decides.

The two channels are asymmetric, and `ShipmentPlan` is the witness. Exposing
down is final and bounded: one decision, whole subtree, nothing ceded - and
no way out, because only an upward exposure could ever carry a symbol out of
its subtree. So `app` is not allowed to import `ShipmentPlan` while
`shipping`'s descendants are: the **parent** is the outsider here, root
included. Select `ShipmentPlan` and the whole story is one short flow and a
single blinking arrival.

## Example 2: Both channels at once

In this smaller app, we keep the pricing rules in a `pricing` module, with
order submission in a `checkout` module beside it - and `PriceModel` is
wanted on both sides of `pricing`'s boundary: by its own sub-modules and by
the app around it.

One owner, one symbol, both exposure decisions. `pricing` owns `PriceModel`
and exposes it to its descendants _and_ to its parent - the first `▲▼`
marker in the series, and it is not a third channel: two independent one-hop
decisions that happen to share a row. The grant reaches `discounts` and
`taxes`; the upward exposure reaches `app`, which composes the type and
stops it.

The lesson is the ceiling. Both channels at once buy exactly the owner's
subtree plus its parent - structurally the most any owner can reach alone.
`checkout`, the sibling that would most plausibly want `PriceModel`, is not
allowed to import it: crossing to a sibling was never `pricing`'s decision
to make, and `app` made none. Select `PriceModel` and both flows march out
of a single row - up one hop, down one subtree - and stop.

Reach ends where decisions end. There are three dots in this picture, all of
them on `pricing` and `checkout`; `app` has none, and both symbols stop
there. The default at every module, everywhere in the model, is "goes no
further" - every hop beyond it is somebody's explicit decision.
(`submitOrder` is a specimen already known from Example 1 - exposed up,
composed, stopped - wearing the same color there and here.)

## Two footnotes

**Grants are uniform.** A descendant grant reaches every branch of the
granter's subtree - including the branch the symbol originally came up
through. No exclusion is possible, and routed symbols carry no provenance. A
family that does not want a symbol reachable from inside its own subtree
resolves that the only way the model offers: by not exposing it upward at
all.

**Subdivision is invisible.** Grants address subtrees, not shapes. Splitting
a module into two sub-modules changes nothing about what either half may
import - structure can be refined freely without renegotiating anybody's
access.

## Where the precision lives

The tree rules on this page are half the complete rule; the other half is on
the [tags](/tags) page, where two more example universes keep the tree
trivial and let a tag be the only variable.

The examples are teaching material. The normative document is the
cross-module importability rules specification, which ships with the
repository at `docs/model/cross-module-importability-rules.md`, with the
vocabulary defined in the [glossary](/glossary). Where a page and the
specification appear to disagree, the specification is right.
